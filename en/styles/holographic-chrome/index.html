Holographic Chrome

全息虹彩 · 铬金属 · Holographic / Iridescent & Chrome

Two reflective material families — spectral iridescence and liquid-metal chrome: holographic gradients sweep across hues to mimic diffraction film, chrome lays down silver with a hard horizon reflection; specular sheen and micro diffraction stripes carry the texture, aimed at cinematic launches, fashion and music.

Parameters
Motion▾
modeholo

JS-type param (the demo rebuilds gradients on change). holo = cross-hue spectral gradient (conic sampling, mimicking diffraction film); chrome = silver liquid metal with a hard dark 'horizon' at 50% of a 180° linear gradient. The two-family split is the structure recorded in daisyUI 2025 entries 014/015 (research 03 §20).

diffractionon

JS-type param (demo subscribes and toggles data-diffraction). on overlays ultra-fine repeating-gradient stripes (micro rainbow) on the card edges, mimicking the 'diffraction effect' — one of the three traits listed verbatim in research 03 §20's summary table (spectral gradient / specular reflection / diffraction).

Geometry▾
hueSpan240

Spectral hue span (degrees) in holo mode. The research lists 'spectral gradients' as a trait without a span value; the 240° default is this entry's baseline — below 360° the ring doesn't close, reading as 'a band of spectrum' rather than a full holographic wheel.

sheen1

Specular-reflection strength multiplier applied to the pointer-driven highlight and the title's glow. Official qualitative referent: WWDC25 session 219 names specular highlights/lensing a material-defining device (research 09 §1.4d/§8 — a 2025-context cousin of this style); multiplier 1 is the baseline, with no value recorded in the research.

Code breakdown

Structure & DOM

Holographic chrome is “a dark stage + one reflective subject”: the card is the protagonist (iridescent title, chrome button, diffraction edges), and a specular highlight follows the pointer across its face — all reflectivity lives on decorative layers (::before or dedicated divs, aria-hidden); semantic content (title/buttons) stays plain text.

<div class="scene">
  <div class="card">
    <div class="orb" aria-hidden="true"></div>       <!-- iridescent ring -->
    <h1 class="holo-title">HOLO</h1>                 <!-- background-clip: text -->
    <button class="chrome-btn">Collect</button>
    <div class="sheen" aria-hidden="true"></div>     <!-- pointer highlight -->
    <div class="diffraction" aria-hidden="true"></div> <!-- diffraction stripes -->
  </div>
</div>

Key points:

  • The two families (holo/chrome) are two gradient recipes on the same card, switched by data-mode — mirroring the two-family structure recorded in daisyUI 2025 entries 014/015;
  • The sheen layer receives pointer coordinates via CSS variables --mx/--my, with pointer-events: none;
  • Diffraction stripes are a repeating-linear-gradient micro-rainbow masked to the card edges (mask-image) so they never cover text.

Styling & Parameters

One CSS variable, three JS-type params:

.demo-stage { --hc-sheen: 1; }
/* holo: a conic spectrum sampled per hue span (JS builds backgroundImage) */
background: conic-gradient(from 210deg, oklch(0.78 0.16 0), …, oklch(0.78 0.16 <span>));
/* chrome: silver with a hard reflection — a dark "horizon" line at 50% */
background: linear-gradient(180deg, #fafbfd 0%, #cfd6e0 42%, #8a93a3 49%,
                            #454b57 50%, #98a1b0 55%, #e8ecf2 78%, #f6f8fb);
/* pointer specular, strength scaled by the sheen multiplier */
.sheen { background: radial-gradient(46% 60% at var(--mx) var(--my),
  oklch(1 0 0 / calc(0.28 * var(--hc-sheen))), transparent 70%); }
/* diffraction stripes: 1px-scale rainbow repeating gradient, opacity 0.16 + screen blend */

Where the numbers come from:

  • The three traits “spectral gradient, specular reflection, diffraction effect” are verbatim from research 03 §20’s summary table; daisyUI 2026’s verbatim scene: “Reflective materials remain useful for cinematic launches, fashion, music, and premium technology”;
  • The 240° span, sheen multiplier 1 and stripe opacity 0.16 are this entry’s baselines (the research records no values);
  • The official qualitative referent for the specular: WWDC25 session 219 names specular highlights/lensing a material-defining device (research 09 §1.4d/§8) — a 2025 Liquid Glass cousin of this style;
  • chrome’s “dark horizon at 50%” is classic liquid-metal rendering (this demo’s recipe, a baseline setting).

Motion & Interaction

Reflective motion means “the light moves, the object doesn’t”:

  • Pointer specular: pointermove writes the cursor position into --mx/--my and the highlight follows instantly — user-driven light needs no duration;
  • Spectral drift: the holo title’s background-position drifts slowly over 9s alternate, mimicking thin-film interference; chrome mode stands still (metal doesn’t shimmer);
  • Ring rotation: the iridescent ring spins linearly over 14s (decoration); buttons rise 1px on hover and sink 1px on press;
  • Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the drift and rotation freeze; static gradients and the pointer highlight remain — half of the light’s “life” is user input, and that half can stay.

Real-world examples

  • Instagram’s 2016 rebrand — the landmark mass-market use of a cross-hue spectral gradient: a magenta→orange→yellow→cyan→blue cone replaced the skeuomorphic camera, still the most famous “spectrum as brand subject” case.
  • Spotify’s yearly Wrapped — every year high-saturation spectral/duo-tone gradient covers and cards flood social feeds; the annual practice of the “music + reflective gradient” pairing (the fashion/music context of daisyUI’s 2026 quote).
  • Apple Card (2019, Apple × Goldman Sachs) — the liquid-metal sheen of a physical titanium card: the real-world prototype of the silver hard-horizon reflection, matching daisyUI’s “premium technology” scene.

Working ranges: spectral band 180–300°, sheen 0.6–1.2. One reflective subject per screen; chrome only for large bold display type (≥24px, the SC 1.4.3 large-text 3:1 rule) — never body text.

When to use 3
  • Cinematic product launches and teaser heroes — daisyUI 2026's verbatim scene 'cinematic launches'
  • Fashion/music/beauty limited drops — reflective material matches the 'premium technology' register (same quote)
  • A single hero element on a dark interface (title/logo/medal) —大面积 use dilutes the scarcity
When not to use 3
  • Body text and form controls — spectral gradients devour text contrast; chrome buttons jitter across >3:1 lightness spans
  • Light/white interfaces — reflective material needs a dark field; iridescence turns muddy on white
  • Accessibility-critical paths (warnings, errors) — semantics must ride on solid color and icons, never on reflective decoration
Accessibility

Check spectral type against the brightest and darkest stops for 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.3), falling back to solid when it fails; chrome's dark 'horizon' at 50% halves glyph lightness mid-letter — reserve it for large bold display (≥24px, the 3:1 large-text rule); the specular sheen is aria-hidden decoration; diffraction stripes stay ≤0.2 alpha and never over text; all reflective motion (glow drift) freezes under prefers-reduced-motion.

Browser support

Chrome and iridescent type depend on background-clip: text (Chromium/Safari have long needed the -webkit- prefix, Firefox 49+ unprefixed; always write both plus a solid fallback); the pointer highlight uses CSS variables + radial-gradient with no prefix needs; diffraction stripes use repeating-linear-gradient at baseline support.

Fallback

Without background-clip: text the title falls back to bright grey solid on dark with a glow; on engines without conic-gradient the spectral band degrades to a two-stop linear gradient; turning diffraction off carries no information loss — all reflectivity is decorative.
