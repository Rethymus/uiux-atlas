Frutiger Aero

Frutiger 美学 · 水色科技自然风 · Aero Glass

The 2005–2013 'nature + tech gloss' aesthetic: brisk sky-blue and grass-green fields, glass bubbles, elliptical specular highlights and watery glass texture — successor to Y2K and the forerunner of the glass era (Glassmorphism/Liquid Glass).

Code breakdown

Structure & DOM

Frutiger Aero is a “nature layer + glass layer” scene: the base is a sky gradient (sky blue → pale aqua) and an elliptical grass hill, topped by a white 15% glass card and bubble buttons — nature supplies the color and mood, glass supplies the operating surface; remove either and the style collapses.

<div class="scene">
  <div class="sun" aria-hidden="true"></div>
  <div class="hill" aria-hidden="true"></div>
  <div class="bubbles" aria-hidden="true">…</div>
  <div class="glass-card">
    <h2>Aqua Player</h2>
    <button class="bubble-btn">▶ Play</button>
  </div>
</div>

Key points:

  • The glass card is a sibling of the backdrop; backdrop-filter blurs the sky and hill showing through, with an @supports fallback that raises fill opacity;
  • The bubble layer is pointer-events: none + aria-hidden and never intercepts interaction;
  • The bubble button’s elliptical highlight lives on ::before, covering only the top 40% — it never invades the text zone.

Styling & Parameters

All four parameters are CSS-variable type, injected via DemoStage:

.demo-stage {
  --fa-sky: #8ecdeb;    /* baseline; the research records no hex */
  --fa-grass: #7dbb4e;
  --fa-gloss: 0.35;     /* inside 09 §1.4d's derived .25~.4 convention (not official) */
  --fa-blur: 20px;      /* Apple's production CSS, grabbed first-hand */
}
.fa-hill {
  border-radius: 50% 50% 0 0;
  background: linear-gradient(to top, deep green, var(--fa-grass) 60%, light green);
}
.fa-card {
  background-color: oklch(1 0 0 / 0.15);
  -webkit-backdrop-filter: blur(var(--fa-blur)) saturate(160%);
  backdrop-filter: blur(var(--fa-blur)) saturate(160%);
}
.fa-btn::before {
  background: linear-gradient(to bottom,
    oklch(1 0 0 / calc(0.55 + 0.45 * var(--fa-gloss))), transparent);
}

Where the numbers come from:

  • blur 20px = grabbed from Apple’s production CSS, the blur(20px)+saturate(180%) baseline tier (research 09 §1.4a; also research 03 §1);
  • glossAlpha 0.35 = a value inside the web-replication convention range rgba(255,255,255,.25~.4) derived in research 09 §1.4d, explicitly marked “derived, not official”;
  • Sky #8ECDEB / grass #7DBB4E = this entry’s baseline settings (research 03 §19 records only the “sky blue / grass green” color semantics, no values; community convention, unverified);
  • backdrop-filter support (Chrome/Edge 76+, Firefox 103+, Safari 9+ prefix, Safari 18 unprefixed) is recorded in research 03 §1.

Motion & Interaction

Aero motion is “the physics of water” — rising, swaying, no sharp corners:

  • Rising bubbles: tapping “Bubbles” spawns decorative bubbles that float up 92vh over 4–7s and fade, removed from the DOM when the animation ends (no node build-up);
  • Press feedback: bubble buttons get translateY(1px) scale(0.98) on :active — like pressing a droplet;
  • Entrance: the glass card fades in with a 14px rise and 0.97 scale over 0.5s, once;
  • Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, bubbles hover statically at 30vh and the entrance is removed — the naturalness stands on static material, not animation.

Real-world examples

  • Windows Vista / 7 Aero glass (2006/2009, Microsoft) — the glass-semantics source recorded in research 03 §1 and the “Aero” in the style’s name: the large-scale commercial start of translucent frames, blurred backdrops and specular highlights.
  • Nintendo Wii channel menu (2006) — a widely accepted product of this aesthetic: white rounded panels, sky-blue field and glossy channel buttons — the generation’s definitive “nature + tech gloss” home-screen work.
  • iOS 6-and-earlier glossy buttons (2007–2013) — the elliptical top-highlight button grammar became mobile UI’s common language in this period, wholesale replaced when iOS 7 turned flat in 2013.

Parameter Sensitivity

ParameterTurning it up…Turning it down…
skyBlue— (keep the hue in the 210–220° band; drifting purple slides into Y2K territory)— (too pale nears white and loses the “clear sky” context; too deep darkens the whole mood)
grassGreenhigher saturation reads “midsummer”, intensifying the nature mood and the warm/cool contrast with glassnear grey-green the nature feel vanishes and the scene degrades into a generic glass-card demo
glossAlphaa brighter sheen, a stronger “droplet” feel; past 0.45 the highlight reads as a white blob and top-edge text contrast tightensthe sheen dies into a translucent capsule; at 0 the “bubble” semantics are only the rounding
blurthicker glass, the sky and hill dissolve into soft color fields; past 30px sampling cost climbsmore backdrop detail shows through — thinner glass; at 0 only the 15% white fill remains

Working ranges: gloss 0.25–0.4 (09 §1.4d’s derived range), blur 14–24px. Keep sky blue light and grass green mid-saturation; body text on glass ≥13px and away from the highlight zone.

When to use 3
  • Health/nature/eco product pages — water, sky and grass semantics fit the subject natively
  • Mass-market lightweight tools and launchers (weather, calendar, player) — big round bubble buttons whose gloss hints pressability
  • Nostalgia marketing and 'millennial childhood' campaigns — Aero is the most recognizable screen memory of the 2005–2013 generation
When not to use 3
  • Dense information panels — gloss and bubble shapes keep draining attention; tables/dashboards can't carry it
  • Developer/terminal tools — the 'nature + gloss' semantics misalign with engineer contexts and hurt credibility
  • Low-contrast warning content — pale text over white elliptical gloss fails contrast easily; warnings need solid fields
Accessibility

Check text on the glass against both the brightest (white gloss) and darkest (grass green) backdrops for 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.3); the specular highlight must never invade the text zone — the higher the gloss alpha, the worse top-edge text contrast; rising bubbles are a purely decorative layer, aria-hidden with pointer-events: none; the no-blur fallback must keep text on a readable field.

Browser support

The glass card depends on backdrop-filter: Chrome/Edge 76+ (2019), Firefox 103+ (2022), Safari 9+ with the -webkit- prefix, unprefixed since Safari 18 (2024-09) (research 03 §1); elliptical highlights and grass hills use radial-gradient at CSS Image Values baseline, no extra prefixes.

Fallback

Without backdrop-filter, raise the glass fill opacity (white ≈0.7) and keep the border and specular highlight — half of Aero's identity is gloss and color, so it survives losing the blur; in the worst case degrade to a solid sky field + grass hill + white rounded card.
