Zero to Glass

6 steps

When you finish you can: write a frosted glass card floating over a colorful backdrop on your own; equip it with M3 content-color state-layer feedback, themed shadow elevation and a press-feedback pair; verify 4.5:1 text contrast with the ratio formula; and assemble the four layers into a deliverable via the Glass Card Pro recipe.

Who it's for Early-career frontend developers who want to turn glassmorphism from a screenshot into a production-grade component they can write, tune and replicate — not just a blur() filter.

Path steps

  1. 01

    Glassmorphism ENTRY

    Why now See the whole picture first: the style's definition, when to use it, and the discipline that glass belongs to controls/nav layers only — the baseline for every later step.

  2. 02

    Backdrop Filter ENTRY

    Why now The physical engine of glass: backdrop-filter's blur/saturate parameters, capability detection and the solid-surface fallback that keeps your glass working everywhere.

  3. 03

    ThemeShadow (Fluent) ENTRY

    Why now A glass card needs elevation semantics: how shadow params tokenize per theme, "small and sharp = near, large and soft = far", and switching levels only when hierarchy truly changes.

  4. 04

    Press Feedback Pair ENTRY

    Why now Custom controls always carry a press state (Apple's rule): why scale 0.96 + an alpha state layer must appear as a pair, and why touch feedback never rides on the glass itself.

  5. 05

    Contrast Ratio Formula ENTRY

    Why now Readability is where glass fails: master the contrast ratio formula to check text-on-glass against the brightest and darkest backdrop extremes for 4.5:1.

  6. 06

    Glass Card Pro RECIPE

    Why now The capstone: the full recipe where four layers each own one job (material ambience, state-layer feedback, shadow elevation, press-pair touch) assembles the previous five steps into a production-grade, one-click-replicable card.

Self-check

Tick each criterion as you verify it; progress is saved in your browser (localStorage), never synced to a server.
