Responsive Images

srcset · sizes · picture 元素 · fetchpriority

srcset/sizes let the browser pick a resolution candidate by 'slot width × DPR'; <picture> handles art direction and format switching; fetchpriority hints the priority of above-the-fold heroes — three tools, one job: half the bytes for the same image.

Code breakdown

Structure & DOM

The three tools split the job; DOM structure follows the use case:

<!-- Resolutions only: official advice is plain img, no picture wrapper (data-saver can downgrade automatically, research 12 §1) -->
<img src="hero.jpg"
     srcset="hero.avif 1200w, hero@2x.avif 2400w"
     sizes="(min-width: 900px) 60vw, 100vw"
     width="1200" height="675" fetchpriority="high" alt="…" />

<!-- Art direction / format switching: picture for explicit control, img as the safety net -->
<picture>
  <source type="image/avif" srcset="hero.avif 1200w, hero@2x.avif 2400w"
          sizes="(min-width: 900px) 60vw, 100vw" />
  <source type="image/webp" srcset="hero.webp 1200w" />
  <img src="hero.jpg" width="1200" height="675" alt="…" />
</picture>

Key points:

  • Each srcset item is URL + descriptor: a 1200w width descriptor or a 2x density descriptor — the two cannot be mixed and duplicate values are disallowed; sizes holds “media condition–slot width” pairs and only applies when srcset uses w descriptors (research 12 §1);
  • Selection rule: with sizes, “slot width × DPR = ideal width”; the browser picks the closest candidate before downloading, falling back to <img src> when nothing matches (research 12 §1);
  • A <source> whose media condition fails, or whose type MIME is unknown, is skipped for the next one — the fallback img always sits last;
  • fetchpriority is a hint, not a command (high/low/auto, default auto); object-fit/object-position must sit on the child <img>, never on <picture> (research 12 §1).

Styling & Parameters

This entry’s “parameters” are attribute-level configuration; the demo drives three JS-type params (the browser’s selection algorithm takes no custom properties):

<img srcset="…640w, …1200w, …2400w"
     sizes="{slot}px"
     fetchpriority="high" width="1200" height="675" />

Where the numbers come from:

  • 60vw, the min-width: 900px breakpoint, the 1200w/2400w candidates and the width="1200" height="675" pairing are verbatim from research 12 §1’s fragment; the demo’s extra 640w grade is a demo addition, unverified (so narrow slots get a visible switch too);
  • The viewport presets 390/768/1280px are common community device anchors, unverified as spec values (390 CSS px phone-class, 768 iPad portrait, 1280 entry desktop);
  • <picture>: Baseline Widely available since 2016-03 (MDN, research 12 §1); fetchpriority: Chrome/Edge 102+, Firefox 132+, Safari 17.2+ (web.dev, research 12 §1).

Motion & Interaction

What “moves” here is the selection algorithm, not an animation:

  • Switching viewport / slot share: the demo rewrites the sizes attribute — a srcset/sizes change is a “relevant mutation”, so the browser re-runs selection and swaps the image live; the demo reads currentSrc every step to show the real pick, never a prediction;
  • DPR joins the math: the readout shows slot × DPR = ideal width, with DPR from the real devicePixelRatio (on 2x screens the ideal doubles and bigger candidates win);
  • The art-direction panel’s media condition is evaluated against the real browser viewport (the simulated one cannot move it) — drag the real window width to see the wide/square swap; the demo labels this difference honestly;
  • fetchpriority only bites during real network loads: the demo writes the attribute and echoes it; research 12 §1 measured Google Flights dropping LCP 2.6s→1.9s with high on its hero, and Oodle saving 2s with low on non-first carousel slides; a near-viewport loading="lazy" image still fetched high should switch to fetchpriority="low";
  • No transition on candidate swaps — an image swap is an instant resource change; a fade would be dishonest.

Real-world examples

  • Google Flights (measured on web.dev, research 12 §1): fetchpriority="high" on the LCP hero took LCP from 2.6s to 1.9s — the case study, together with the “switch lazy images still fetched high to low” rule, lives in web.dev’s fetch-priority article;
  • Oodle’s carousel (measured on web.dev, research 12 §1): fetchpriority="low" on non-first slides cut page load by 2s — the origin of the “first slide high, the rest low” recipe;
  • MDN’s <picture> three-scenario taxonomy (research 12 §1): art direction / format switching / capability-based picks is the official classification, and the AVIF→WebP→jpg source ordering is the documented shape (editorial combination — per-site adoption lists not individually verified).

Parameter Sensitivity

ParameterTurning it up…Turning it down…
viewporta wider slot pushes the ideal width up, so bigger candidates win (bytes for sharpness)a narrower slot picks smaller candidates (sharpness for bytes); the hit label tracks it live

Rules of thumb: double the candidate sizes (1200w→2400w) rather than spreading evenly; derive sizes breakpoints from layout slots, not device model numbers; width/height attributes plus a CSS-ratio slot give zero shift before and after load.

When to use 4
  • Above-the-fold LCP heroes: multi-density srcset + fetchpriority="high" + explicit width/height (research 12 §1 fragment and the Google Flights measurement)
  • Art direction: wide crop on desktop, square on phones — <source media> swaps composition, not just scale
  • Format switching: AVIF/WebP sources first with a jpg fallback — savings on new engines, unbroken on old
  • Carousel/long-list non-hero images: fetchpriority="low" (+ loading="lazy") — Oodle measured a 2s saving, research 12 §1
When not to use 4
  • Wrapping <picture> when you only have resolutions — the official advice is plain img srcset so data-saver can downgrade automatically (research 12 §1)
  • Mixing w and x descriptors or repeating a value in srcset — the two cannot be mixed and duplicates are disallowed (research 12 §1)
  • Putting object-fit on <picture> — it belongs on the child img (research 12 §1)
  • fetchpriority="high" on everything — maxing every hint is no hint at all; only the LCP image earns it (research 12 §1 semantics)
Accessibility

Candidate switching never changes semantics: whichever candidate the browser picks, the alt is written once on the img and describes the image (not the filename or size); inside <picture> the img is the sole alt carrier, sources stay out of the accessibility tree; decorative images get alt=""; the demo's device frame is a mockup, with real readouts (slot/DPR/chosen candidate) presented as text, never color-only.

Browser support

<picture> is Baseline Widely available since 2016-03 across mainstream browsers (MDN, research 12 §1); srcset/sizes landed in the same era. fetchpriority: Chrome/Edge 102+, Firefox 132+, Safari 17.2+ (web.dev, research 12 §1). Unsupported browsers ignore the attribute and load at default priority, without errors.

Fallback

<picture>'s compatibility fallback is built into the structure: the <img> without type/media is the final safety net; browsers skip sources whose media condition fails or whose MIME they don't know; ancient browsers without srcset/sizes just load the img src (ship a suitably-sized jpg there); unrecognized fetchpriority is silently ignored — so 'make the fallback image good enough' is a structural discipline.

Sources 2

OriginFrom the HTML responsive-images mechanism (research 12 §1, verified first-hand on MDN/web.dev): when only offering multiple resolutions, the official advice is plain <img srcset> without <picture> (so data-saver modes can auto-pick lower density); <picture> covers three explicit-control scenarios — art direction per viewport, format switching (AVIF/WebP fallback), and capability-based picks; fetchpriority is a hint about a resource's relative priority (high/low/auto, default auto), mirrored by the Fetch API's priority option.
