Pareto Principle

80/20 法则 · 80-20 Rule · 二八法则

Roughly 80% of effects come from roughly 20% of causes — and 'roughly' is part of the law. Identify the vital few areas that yield the most and concentrate effort there; never apply it mechanically.

Parameters

skew1.9

JS-type param: distribution skew (demo teaching model, share ∝ 1/rank^skew, normalized). At the default 1.9, the top 2 of 10 features carry ≈78.4% (computed live in the demo) — reproducing the 'roughly 80/20' shape. The law itself is the qualitative 'roughly 80/20' claim and fixes no distribution parameter.

budget2项
项

JS-type param: the 'polish these' engineering budget — the demo highlights the top-budget features by payoff and shows their covered share plus the remainder. The default 2 matches the '20% vital few'; raising it to 4 moves coverage from ≈78% to ≈86% — the flattening gain is the quantitative case for concentrating on the vital few.

Structure & DOM

The demo is a feature-tiering chart: a 10-feature usage-share bar chart + cumulative curve + an 80% reference line, with a side panel carrying three live numbers.

<svg viewBox="0 0 380 210">
  <line class="pp-ref" x1="34" y1="58" x2="350" y2="58" />  <!-- 80% reference line -->
  <rect class="pp-bar pp-bar-hi" x="38" y="52" width="22" height="118" />  <!-- budgeted highlight -->
  <rect class="pp-bar" x="69" y="131" width="22" height="39" />
  <path class="pp-cum" d="M49,88 L80,124 …" />              <!-- cumulative curve -->
</svg>
<p class="pp-inline" aria-live="polite">
  Top 2 carry 78.4% · 80% line at top-3 · budget of 2 covers 78.4%
</p>

Key points:

  • 10 features line the x-axis ordered by payoff, shares computed live and normalized from share ∝ 1/rank^skew — every percentage comes from the current params; not a single “80” is hard-coded;
  • The conclusion is dual-channel: beyond the chart, “top 2 carry X% / 80% line at top-k / budget covers Y%” is permanent text (aria-live synced), and the panel lists the top-5 names with shares;
  • The budget highlight is dual-channel: bars change color AND each listed item carries a literal “budgeted” tag — never color alone.

  • skew defaults to 1.9: the top 2 of 10 features then carry ≈78.4% (computed live), reproducing the ‘roughly 80/20’ shape; this is a demo teaching-model param — the law itself is only the qualitative ‘roughly 80/20’ claim (lawsofux stresses roughly, not an exact split);
  • budget defaults to 2: the ‘20% vital few’; raising it to 4 covers ≈86% — the gain flattens from ~78%→~86%, and that diminishing-returns curve is the quantitative case for concentration;
  • Numbers from the source: Pareto’s observation that roughly 80% of Italy’s land belonged to 20% of its population, the UX phrasing ‘concentrate energy on the areas that bring the greatest benefit to the most users’, and the misuse warning ‘mechanical application (e.g. obsessing over click counts)’ — all from supplement research 12 §14;
  • The 80% reference line sits on a right-side 0–100% cumulative axis; findIndex(c >= 80) yields ‘the top-k features needed to reach 80%’.

Motion & Interaction

  • Dragging skew: bars and the cumulative curve re-lay smoothly via transition: y/height/d 0.3s ease (SVG geometry props; where unsupported they snap instantly — no compat risk);
  • Dragging budget: the highlighted set switches instantly and the panel’s ‘budgeted’ tags follow;
  • Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce both transitions drop and numbers update instantly;
  • The SVG carries role="img" with a dynamic aria-label (top-2 share, budget coverage, 80%-line rank) so the conclusion is available to screen readers without vision.

Real-world examples

  • Analytics-driven feature tiering (generic practice): telemetry/funnels surface the top-20% features for concentrated polish — the literal application of supplement research 12 §14’s ‘concentrate energy on the areas that bring the greatest benefit to the most users’; this demo’s ‘rank by payoff + budget’ is that workflow, taught;
  • Toolbar / primary-menu layering (generic practice): high-frequency actions stay in primary spots, low-frequency power tucks into ‘more’ menus — the interface-level 80/20, complementary to the serial-position rule ‘key actions at the far ends’ (research 07 §29): one splits by how much, the other by where;
  • Misuse warning (supplement research 12 §14): lawsofux names the mechanical application — proxy goals like ‘minimizing click counts’ instead of the payoff itself; 80/20 is a diagnostic lens, not a scalpel for amputating the long tail.

Parameter Sensitivity

ParameterTurning it up…Turning it down…
skew3.0: a steeper distribution pushes the top-2 share to ~93% — the extreme ‘head-concentrated’ shape, 80% line at just top-21.5: a flatter distribution drops the top-2 share to ~67%, the 80% line retreats to top-4 — in a flat world concentration loses its punch
budget10: covers 100% but spreads effort evenly; contrast with the 78% of 2 items to see how disproportionate the returns are2: exactly the ‘20% budget’ at ~78% coverage — the slider stops there (min=2)

The lesson: find the vital 20% with data first, then concentrate the polish budget there; 80/20 is a ‘roughly’ approximation — use it to rank priorities, not to make exact promises, and never to mechanically amputate strategically vital long-tail features (supplement research 12 §14).

When to use 3
  • Release planning: identify the top-20% features by usage/payoff and prioritize their polish, performance and accessibility
  • Interface layering: keep high-frequency actions in primary spots, tuck low-frequency power into 'more' menus
  • Performance & quality spend: optimize the head paths serving the most users before the long tail
When not to use 3
  • Mechanical 80/20: lawsofux names the misuse as mechanical proxies like 'minimize click counts' instead of the payoff itself
  • Reporting 80/20 as exact or hard-coding it — 'roughly' is part of the law's own wording
  • Amputating low-usage but strategically vital long-tail features (compliance, accessibility, enterprise must-haves)
Accessibility

Chart information must be dual-channel: beyond the SVG, 'top 2 carry X%', '80% line reached at k features' and 'budget covers Y% · remainder Z%' all appear as text updating live with the params; the budget highlight is marked by color AND the literal word 'budgeted', never color-only; the SVG carries role=img with a dynamic aria-label; under prefers-reduced-motion bar and curve transitions drop and numbers update instantly.

Browser support

The bar chart and cumulative curve are inline SVG; all modern browsers.

Fallback

The engineering exit: rank features by analytics (usage/payoff) and budget toward the head. Degradation path: without telemetry, proxy with last iteration's high-frequency feature list or the top-N support tickets; with no ranking basis at all, at least hand-polish the head path (home → core task) first. Red line: 80/20 is approximate — never mechanically amputate strategically vital long-tail features; lawsofux names the misuse exactly as 'mechanical application (e.g. obsessing over click counts)'.
