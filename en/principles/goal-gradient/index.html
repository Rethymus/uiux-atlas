Goal-Gradient Effect

目标梯度 · Goal-Gradient

Effort toward a goal intensifies as the goal gets closer. Give clear progress indicators, pre-seed 'artificial progress' at the start, and invest in the final stretch — the nearer the goal, the stronger each step's pull.

Code breakdown

Structure & DOM

The demo is a playable coffee punch card: stamp slots, a progress bar, a punch button, plus a per-stamp interval panel.

<div class="gg-card">
  <div class="gg-stamps">
    <span class="gg-stamp" data-pre="true">pre</span>  <!-- head-start artificial progress, honestly labeled -->
    <span class="gg-stamp" data-hit="true">✓</span>   <!-- stamps the user earned -->
    <span class="gg-stamp"></span>                     <!-- empty slot -->
  </div>
  <div class="gg-progress" role="progressbar"><i style="width: 40%"></i></div>
  <p class="gg-readout" aria-live="polite">4/10 · 40% · 6 stamps left</p>
  <button class="gg-punch">Punch</button>
</div>
<div class="gg-panel">…per-stamp interval bars + latest/min/max text…</div>

Key points:

  • The three slot states differ on glyph + shape + color channels at once: empty dashed circle, user-stamped solid with ✓, pre-stamped marked with the pre glyph and honestly labeled as artificial progress — never color-only (illusory progress must be honest; that is the ethical edge of Kivetz et al.’s research);
  • The conclusion never relies on reading a chart: ‘n/N · percent · m stamps left’ text syncs with aria-live, and every interval bar carries its numeric value;
  • On completion the punch button morphs in place into ‘another round’ — the next goal starts the instant one ends, keeping the gradient loop unbroken.

Styling & Parameters

// steps / headStart are JS-type params subscribed from the store; intervals are real millisecond measurements
const prev = punches === 0 ? roundStart : roundStart + intervals.reduce((a, b) => a + b, 0);
setIntervals((arr) => [...arr, Math.round(now - prev)]);
  • steps defaults to 10 stamps (a typical reward card’s size), headStart to 1 — pre-punching one ‘done’ slot, from the signature trick “pre-stamp one completed slot on steppers/reward cards” (supplement research 12 §16-9);
  • Both params are demo teaching params: the law says only “closer → faster”, nothing about card size or how many slots to pre-punch — stated as such in the schema;
  • Interval = real milliseconds since the previous stamp (stamp 1 measured from round start) — the demo measures the user’s own behavior, no simulated data;
  • Bar heights normalize as interval / maxInterval, bar order = stamp order, so ‘opening vs final stretch’ is directly comparable by eye;
  • The progress bar uses transition: width 0.3s ease, matching the site-wide percent-done convention (NN/g: percent-done is mandatory past 10s, research 07 §20).

Motion & Interaction

  • Each click: the new stamp pops once over 0.25s (gg-pop), the bar slides 0.3s, interval bars re-lay over 0.3s — all confined to transform/opacity/width;
  • On reaching the goal: a one-shot “Done! reward unlocked ✓” line plus the button turning green as ‘another round’; the celebration fires once, never loops;
  • Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce all three animations/transitions drop and numbers update instantly — timing and text are unaffected by the motion setting;
  • Keyboard-ready: punch is a native button (Space/Enter); each slot carries its own aria-label.

Real-world examples

  • The coffee loyalty card’s pre-punched first stamp: the ‘artificial progress’ pattern recorded in supplement research 12 §14, applied literally — a free done-stamp at the start puts users at non-zero progress immediately, measurably shortening the opening interval (supplement research 12 §16-9);
  • Uber-style head-start progress: the application form named verbatim in supplement research 12 §14 — a small instantly-completed segment at the start of a flow drops users into the “already close” mindset;
  • NN/g’s percent-done progress bars: operations past 10 seconds must show percentage progress (research 07 §20) — the loading progress line is the goal-gradient’s carrier at the system-feedback layer; the clearer the closing signal, the more tolerable the wait.

Parameter Sensitivity

ParameterTurning it up…Turning it down…
steps12 stamps: a longer middle makes the motivation dip more visible — the “long goal with no milestones” caution, playable6 stamps: the final sprint arrives sooner and the interval narrowing shows earlier
headStart3 stamps: you open at 30% progress and the very first interval is visibly shorter — artificial progress’s pull on the opening step, directly measurable0 stamps: the no-head-start control; alternate rounds at 0 and 1 and compare stamp-1 intervals to reproduce the illusory-progress effect on yourself

The lesson: progress must be clear (visible), honest (pre-stamped slots disclosed), and finish-visible (m stamps left always present); investing in the final stretch yields the most — the nearer the goal, the stronger the same step pulls (supplement research 12 §14).

When to use 3
  • Loyalty cards, stamp collecting, points systems: pre-stamp one slot (artificial progress) and concentrate rewards on the final stretch
  • Multi-step flows (sign-up/checkout/onboarding): a clear stepper + percentage makes 'two steps left' visible
  • Waiting: percent-done progress bars for ≥10s operations (NN/g, research 07 §20) — the progress line is itself a goal-gradient carrier
When not to use 3
  • Misreporting progress: pre-stamped slots must be disclosed as artificial progress; passing remaining work off as done is deception (the ethical edge of illusory-progress research)
  • Hiding progress until a final reveal — the gradient needs a continuously felt 'getting closer'
  • One huge goal with no milestones (e.g. an empty 0→100% bar): no near goal, no acceleration
Accessibility

Progress must be dual-channel: beyond the stamp slots, 'n/N collected · percent · m stamps left' and every punch interval appear as text and are announced via aria-live; stamps are never color-only (collected slots carry a ✓ glyph); each interval bar has its numeric value; under prefers-reduced-motion the stamp animation and bar transitions drop and numbers update instantly.

Browser support

The demo uses only native DOM/CSS/JS (div stamp slots, buttons, transitions) — no cutting-edge features; any modern browser runs it.

Fallback

The engineering exit: progress must be 'clear + honest + with a visible finish'. Degradation path: no CSS feature to fall back on; when real progress data is unavailable, pre-seed completed steps per Kivetz et al.'s artificial progress (always disclosed, never misstate what remains); on surfaces with no progress indicator at all, at least state 'N steps left' as text. Mind NN/g's layering: indicators only past 1s, percent-done mandatory past 10s (research 07 §20).
