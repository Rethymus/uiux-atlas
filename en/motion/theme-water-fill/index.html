Theme Water Fill

water fill · liquid fill · 注水进度 · 液面指示 · wave fill · 水位指示

Progress or theme depth told as a rising/falling liquid level: a two-sine surface (1:1.618 frequency ratio for the beat effect) swept through an SVG wave path, clipped by a vessel ring; reduced-motion degrades to a static level.

Web Standard ★★★
Live demo official default
Parameters
Geometry▾
amplitude8px
px

Primary wave amplitude A1, default 8px from research §6.1 (A1=8/A2=3); the demo layers the secondary at the fixed ratio A2=A1×3/8. Higher reads stormier, but in small vessels (A ≈ 1/6 of the diameter) crests can poke past the clip ring; toward 2px the surface nears still water — legal, but the 'living water' feel is gone.

wavelength120px
px

Primary wavelength λ1 (wavenumber k1=2π/λ1). Default 120px = the 'λ≈width/2' tier of §6.1 for the demo vessel (~240px diameter; the /2 and /3 tiers scale with vessel width); the demo layers the secondary at λ2=λ1×2/3. Higher makes sparse crests and a tilting surface; below 80px it turns choppy in a small vessel.

duration500ms
ms

The level transition between the two depth tiers — the demo stage's actual data-fx-duration. Default 500ms aligns with M3's duration-by-area large-container tier (research 20 §5); the wave phase itself keeps translating (ambient), independent of this transition. Higher pours more calmly; below 300ms the level change loses its sense of process.

deepLevel75%
%

The level mapped to the dark theme (a demo design value, not a research-verified one — 'level as theme depth' is the design inference from research §8 candidate 5). Switching to dark pours toward this tier; higher fills the 'deep water' further — at 95% crests graze the ring edge, mind the margin.

shallowLevel35%
%

The level mapped to the light theme (demo design value, same caveat). Switching to light drains toward this tier — 'shallow water = light theme' follows the same direction as the Helmholtz analogy (big cavity → low → dark, small cavity → high → light, research 20 §3.2's physical analogy; the perceptual mapping is unverified). Keep it below deepLevel or the deep/shallow story inverts.

Code breakdown

Structure & DOM

A quantity expressed as a liquid level: a liquid layer clipped by a ring vessel, its surface sampled from a two-sine superposition into one closed path with the phase continuously translating; level tiers (not a completion arc) carry theme depth / volume semantics (research 20 §2.6/§6.1):

<div class="vessel">
  <svg aria-hidden="true">
    <defs>
      <clipPath id="vessel-clip"><circle r="110"/></clipPath><!-- outer ring clip -->
    </defs>
    <path class="water" clip-path="url(#vessel-clip)"/><!-- per-frame surface path -->
    <circle class="ring"/><!-- vessel stroke -->
  </svg>
</div>

Key points:

  • The surface path is per-frame geometry: η(x,t) = A1·sin(k1x−ω1t) + A2·sin(k2x−ω2t+φ) sampled along x into a polyline, closed with a bled bottom edge — the phase term −ωt is the “horizontal cyclic translation”; it is unaffected by the url() clip-path non-interpolability limit (§1.2) and needs neither startViewTransition nor filters;
  • The two-sine superposition’s physical source (Wikipedia, Dispersion (water waves)): two sine waves of slightly different wavelength superpose into beats/wave groups; the demo takes the incommensurable frequency ratio 1:1.618, so the composite never exactly repeats — the mechanical looping of a single sine is gone;
  • Quantity semantics: a liquid level is a spatial metaphor for volume (capacity, depth, remaining), not task completion — completion-arc semantics belong to motion/progress-motion;
  • The level value rides a text readout too; the waves are enhancement only (HIG: motion never the sole channel);
  • The pure-CSS precedents (fill-water-animation / Water-Wave-Text-Animation) translate two background wave layers instead of summing — a viable route, but the arithmetic two-sine superposition requires per-frame path rebuilds.

Styling & Parameters

All five parameters are JS-type; the demo subscribes to the store and drives the frame loop and level transitions:

// Surface sampling (the §6.1 recipe skeleton; A/λ tiers are research-recorded values)
const surf = (x, t) =>
  A1 * Math.sin(k1 * x - w1 * t) +            // primary, k1 = 2π/λ1
  A2 * Math.sin(k2 * x - w2 * t + phi);       // secondary: A2=A1×3/8, λ2=λ1×2/3, ω2/ω1=1.618
// Per frame: M origin → sampled polyline along x → bled bottom closure; mean level = cy+R − level%·2R

Where the numbers come from:

  • A1=8px, A2=3px (3/8), λ≈width/2 and /3 (2:3), angular-speed ratio 1.618 = research §6.1 recorded values; wavenumber k=2π/λ;
  • ω1=2π (1s primary period) is a demo tuning choice — research verified the ratios, not an absolute angular speed;
  • The 500ms level transition = M3’s duration-by-area large-container tier (§5); the easing is §6.3’s liquid curve cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 0.2, 1);
  • deepLevel 75% / shallowLevel 35% are demo design values — “level as theme depth” comes from research §8’s candidate list (a design inference), not a verified figure; the ‘dark = deep = low’ direction follows the Helmholtz analogy (bigger cavity → lower pitch, §3.2’s physical analogy; the crossmodal perceptual mapping is unverified);
  • Amplitude discipline: keep A at 1/8–1/12 of the vessel diameter, or crests poke past the clip ring or expose the sub-surface bottom.

Motion & Interaction

  • Click → pour/drain: the level eases from the current tier toward the target tier (duration = duration, data-fx-duration matches), and the theme commits atomically the instant the level lands; the wave phase is a continuous ambient animation and does not count into that duration — rAF auto-pauses when the page is hidden;
  • Re-click mid-transition: the old run finishes atomically (the level lands on its previous target, theme commits) and the new one plays in full — never frozen or half-way;
  • Reduced motion (the spec’s stated degradation): under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the phase freezes into a static liquid level (one motionless wave frame) and the level lands instantly (duration treated as 0, data-fx-duration synced to 0);
  • Degradation: without rAF/SVG, show a static flat-surface level; the level readout never depends on the animation, so information loses nothing;
  • Text-fill variant: swap the ring clip for a text clip (the Water-Wave-Text-Animation technique) so a headline “floods” as the level rises — the same surface formula, only the clipping layer changes.

Real-world examples

  • coiger/fill-water-animation (22★, “Fill wavy water in circle with pure css code”): a pure-CSS in-circle water fill — the translating-two-wave-layers technique precedent (verified via GitHub API; low-star, noted as technique corroboration rather than authority, research 20 §4);
  • codeaashu/Water-Wave-Text-Animation (20★, HTML/CSS water-wave text): corroboration for the text-clipped variant — evidence the text-clip route works and resonates (verified via GitHub API, same caveat);
  • A public product-grade web implementation of “level as theme depth” was not found (research 20 §4 unverified list) — the demo’s deep/shallow tiers are a demonstration of the design inference, not a stand-in for a product precedent.

Working ranges: A = 1/8–1/12 of the diameter, λ = 1/2–2/3 of the vessel width, duration 400–600ms, a deep/shallow gap ≥ 30%; keep the frequency ratio incommensurable like 1:1.618 — integer ratios make the waveform periodically repeat itself.

When to use 4
  • Theme tier indicator: the level expresses the current theme's 'depth' (dark = deep water, light = shallow), pouring/draining on switch (research §8 candidate 5)
  • Volume/remaining indicators: tanks, battery, memory, quota — quantity as a spatial metaphor, not task completion (complements progress-motion)
  • Branded loading/pouring moments: upload, import, brewing narratives
  • Text-fill variant: clip by text instead of a ring (Water-Wave-Text-Animation technique), headlines 'flooded' as the level rises
When not to use 4
  • Liquid levels for task completion — arcs/percentages carry task semantics; a level expresses 'quantity', and mixing them makes users read remaining volume as progress (same-family caveat as motion/progress-motion)
  • Showing undulating liquid to reduced-motion users — the spec degrades to a static level; large moving surfaces are vestibular triggers (WCAG 2.3.3/C39)
  • Keeping amplitude near the vessel radius — crests poke past the clip ring or expose the bottom; hold A at 1/8–1/12 of the diameter
  • Integer frequency ratios (e.g. 1:2) — the superposition periodically repeats itself (the beat effect dies); keep incommensurable ratios like 1:1.618 (§2.6)
Accessibility

The level value rides a synchronous text readout (this demo shows the live level in a chip); the waves are enhancement only — a liquid level must never be the sole carrier of a quantity (HIG: Make motion optional). Theme state rides data-fx-theme and color. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce → phase frozen, static level, the level lands instantly (the spec's stated degradation); the waves are continuous ambient animation and rAF auto-pauses when the page is hidden, so nothing spins idle per frame. For rapid toggling honor HIG's 'avoid motion on frequent interactions': the level transition is interruptible and finishes atomically under re-click, never queueing.

Browser support

rAF + per-frame SVG path d + clipPath clipping work in every browser — no startViewTransition, no filters (S5 disproved filters on transition pseudo-elements; this entry never touches them). The surface path is per-frame geometry, unaffected by the url() clip-path non-interpolability limit (research 20 §1.2). A pure-CSS route (two translating background waves) is also viable (the precedent repos do exactly that), but the arithmetic two-sine superposition (this entry) requires per-frame path rebuilds; the wave phase is a continuous ambient animation and rAF auto-pauses when the page is hidden.

Fallback

prefers-reduced-motion: reduce → freeze the wave phase and degrade to a static level (render one motionless wave frame, drop the level instantly to the target tier, duration treated as 0, data-fx-duration synced to 0) — the spec's stated degradation. Without rAF/SVG → a static flat-surface level; the numeric level rides a text readout, so information never depends on the animation (HIG: motion never the sole channel). Versus the progress-motion entry: a liquid level is a spatial metaphor for 'quantity', a completion arc is for 'progress' — prefer this entry for volume-like indicators and arcs for task completion.

Sources 3

OriginThe wave math is verified against Wikipedia's 'Dispersion (water waves)': two sine waves of slightly different wavelength superpose into beats/wave groups — the physics behind a 'primary + secondary sine, each advancing at its own speed' surface; design-wise, taking an incommensurable 1:1.618 frequency ratio approximates real water (research 20 §2.6). The recipe skeleton follows §6.1: η(x,t) = A1·sin(k1x−ω1t) + A2·sin(k2x−ω2t+φ) sampled into a closed polyline, with A1=8px, A2=3px (3/8 ratio), λ≈width/2 and /3 (2:3 ratio), angular-speed ratio 1.618; the value is expressed as a 'liquid level' rather than a completion arc, complementing motion/progress-motion. Pure-CSS precedents: coiger/fill-water-animation (22★) and codeaashu/Water-Wave-Text-Animation (20★, verified via GitHub API; low-star, noted as technique corroboration).
