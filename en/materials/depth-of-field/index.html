Depth of Field

景深层次 · Depth of Field · Blur Layering · 模糊分层

Fake photographic depth of field with blur layering: the foreground stays sharp while the far plane loses definition via blur+saturate and a light dim — a blur → dim → sharp depth stack with a switchable focus plane (adjustable blur px).

Community ★★
Live demo official default
Parameters
Geometry▾
blur8px
px

Blur radius applied to out-of-focus planes (filter: blur). Default 8px is the research 12 §8 recipe (.stage__far blur(8px)); the glass midplane's backdrop-filter stays fixed at 6px from the same recipe — each layer has its own job: the far plane follows this param, the glass plane does not. blur acts on the element itself (unlike backdrop-filter, which acts on what is behind it).

saturation0.9

Desaturation factor for out-of-focus planes (saturate()). Default 0.9 is the research 12 §8 recipe (saturate(.9)) — a slight fade pushes the far plane back, building distance alongside the blur; 1 = blur without the fade.

Motion▾
focusnear

JS-type param; the demo subscribes and toggles data-focus (the focus plane). near = sharp foreground (the default photographic semantics; mid/far planes defocus); mid = the glass midplane becomes the subject (itself unblurred, far/near defocus); far = sharp far plane (reversed depth of field, everything in front defocuses — for comparison). Defocused planes get the recipe's blur+saturate+scale(1.02) plus a 25% black veil (borrowing MDN's typical ::backdrop value; demo approximation).

Code breakdown

Structure & DOM

Depth of field is a stack of three planes, each holding its own filter, with the focus plane carrying none: far (the scene), mid (a glass/scrim plane), near (the subject). Depth reads as “blur → dim → sharp”: defocused planes first lose definition to blur, then luminance to a veil, while the foreground owns the sharpness (as defined in research 12 §8).

<div class="stage">
  <div class="stage__far">…full-bleed scene…</div>     <!-- filter: blur+saturate, defocused -->
  <div class="stage__glass">…glass midplane…</div>   <!-- backdrop-filter + scrim base -->
  <div class="stage__near">…info card…</div>      <!-- sharp focus subject -->
</div>

Key points:

  • filter: blur() acts on the element itself, backdrop-filter on the content behind it — the far plane uses the former, the glass plane the latter; never mix them up;
  • Defocused layers need scale(1.02) to compensate edges: blur pulls the rim inward by a ring, and without compensation a hard edge shows (the recipe includes it);
  • A defocused plane must carry no required information — it has given up definition, so information lives only on the focus plane.

Styling & Parameters

Two CSS-variable parameters (blur/saturation, applied only to defocused planes) plus one JS focus switch:

/* Defocused plane: blur + desaturation + edge compensation (research recipe: .stage__far) */
.stage__far {
  filter: blur(var(--dof-blur)) saturate(var(--dof-sat));  /* defaults 8px / 0.9 */
  transform: scale(1.02);                                   /* edge compensation, no layout shift */
}
/* The dim veil: the dim step, 25% black (borrowing MDN's typical ::backdrop value; demo approximation) */
.stage__far::after { background: rgb(0 0 0 / 25%); }

/* Glass midplane: fixed recipe values, does not follow the blur param (.stage__glass) */
.stage__glass {
  -webkit-backdrop-filter: blur(6px);
  backdrop-filter: blur(6px);
  background: color-mix(in oklab, black 55%, transparent);  /* scrim base */
}

Where the numbers come from:

  • blur(8px) + saturate(.9) + scale(1.02) = the far-plane recipe in research 12 §8 (.stage__far);
  • The glass midplane’s backdrop-filter: blur(6px) + color-mix(in oklab, black 55%, transparent) scrim = the same section’s glass recipe (.stage__glass) — the two blurs have separate jobs;
  • The 25% dim veil = MDN’s typical ::backdrop value rgb(0 0 0 / 25%) (same section), used as a demo approximation for layer dimming;
  • filter is Baseline since 2016-09; backdrop-filter/transform are modern baselines.

Motion & Interaction

  • No filter transition on focus switches: frame-by-frame animation of large-area blur has significant GPU-compositing cost, and the research explicitly warns to “avoid large areas and frequently animated elements” (research 12 §8) — the demo swaps the focus plane directly;
  • If a rack-focus animation is truly needed: keep it to small elements, or pre-render both states and cross-dissolve via opacity;
  • The dim veil may fade over ≤0.25s of opacity (a cheap compositable property); disabled under prefers-reduced-motion;
  • will-change goes on when used and comes off when done (MDN best practice, research 09 §9.2) — a permanent hint costs memory and layering;
  • The focus switch itself is input-driven (the user picks the plane), producing no autonomous animation.

Real-world examples

  • iOS 26 scroll edge effects: as content scrolls under glass it “gradually dissolves the content into the background” (WWDC25 219 [532–599s], research 09 §8.7) — “dissolving” is the system-level, definition-loss counterpart of blur layering;
  • MDN’s <dialog> examples: ::backdrop paired with backdrop-filter: blur(0.5rem) over the dimming scrim (research 09 §7.1) — the canonical blur-plus-dim combination behind dialogs, i.e. the production form of this entry’s midplane and veil;
  • Apple HIG material thickness semantics: “thicker materials are more opaque and give better contrast to fine-stroke text; thinner ones are more translucent and preserve the backdrop’s context” (research 09 §1.2) — the official framing of the blur/dim/sharp tradeoff: when a layer yields definition for hierarchy, the foreground must stay readable.

Parameter Sensitivity

ParameterTurning it up…Turning it down…
blurthe far plane recedes further; past 16px it smears into a color field, sampling cost climbs, and the depth cue is lostthe far plane stays legible; under 4px the depth cue vanishes and only dimming remains
saturationbelow 0.7 the far plane reads like a faded photo — pushed further back, but too much reads “dead”1 = blur without the fade, and the sense of distance leans entirely on blur
focus → farreversed depth of field: the foreground defocuses and the far plane sharpens — check whether the abandoned foreground actually carried informationnear is the default photographic semantics: foreground sharp, everything else recedes
The veil (demo approximation)defocused layers grow darker and the “dim” step heavier; too heavy swallows the far plane’s color memoryonly a “bright blur” remains and the three depth steps degrade to two

Working ranges: blur 6–12px (default 8 is the recipe), saturation 0.85–0.95; the glass plane stays fixed at 6px and does not follow. Two hard limits: no required information on defocused planes, and no frame-by-frame animation of large-area blur — break either one and depth of field turns from a material language into a performance incident.

When to use 4
  • Media detail pages and product shelves: the far photo recedes in blur while the info card floats in the sharp layer
  • Hero background softening: a full-bleed backdrop blurred + desaturated so title and CTA own the sharpness
  • The blur-plus-dim pairing behind dialogs: MDN's own ::backdrop blur(0.5rem) example (research 09 §7.1)
  • A reference for scroll edge effects: content 'dissolving into the background' as it scrolls under glass (WWDC25 219, research 09 §8.7) — dissolving is definition loss
When not to use 4
  • Frame-by-frame animation of large-area blur — significant GPU cost, explicitly warned against (research 12 §8); use cross-dissolve for rack focus
  • Readable content on a defocused plane — once a layer carries text/data it must not be blurred (information is not decoration)
  • Very large blur (>16px) over large layers — it smears into color fields, sampling cost climbs, and the depth cue is lost
  • Depth of field as the only hierarchy cue — depth needs size/position/text alongside it (WCAG 1.4.11 boundary legibility)
Accessibility

Depth of field is a purely decorative depth cue: never the sole carrier of hierarchy or state (pair with position/size/text); defocused planes must carry no required information; the blur layer's edge grows outward via scale(1.02), causing no layout shift (no CLS); for vestibular users the focus switch has no filter animation (a direct swap), and all transitions stop under prefers-reduced-motion; the dim veil carries no state semantics.

Browser support

filter is Baseline since 2016-09 (research 12 §8); backdrop-filter and transform are modern baselines. The demo's color-mix() needs Baseline 2023-05 (Chrome 111+ / Safari 16.2+ / Firefox 113+, research 12 §11). blur/saturate over large imagery is a GPU-compositing cost — fine when static, caution when animated (see fallback).

Fallback

Without color-mix(), the veil falls back to rgba(0 0 0 / 25%); on engines without filter, drop the depth-of-field effect and keep hierarchy (dimming + foreground) — the effect is a purely decorative depth cue, so degradation costs nothing functional. Never animate large-area blur frame by frame: filters composite on the GPU and may create new compositing layers at significant cost (research 12 §8); if a rack-focus animation is truly needed, cross-dissolve two states via opacity instead.
