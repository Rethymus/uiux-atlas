Backdrop Filter

背景滤镜 · 背景模糊 · backdrop-filter

Applies a chain of filter functions to everything *behind* the element — blur + saturate + brightness, the web foundation under frosted glass and most system glass materials.

Web Standard ★★★
Live demo official default
Parameters
Geometry▾
blur20px
px

Backdrop blur radius — the direct source of the frosted 'thickness'. Default 20px is the glassmorphism.com generator's classic recipe (research 06 §IV §16); Apple's global nav measures the same 20px in production CSS (superdesign.dev).

saturate180%
%

Saturation compensation for the transmitted backdrop: frosting desaturates what shows through, so raising saturate restores vividness. Default 180% is the production-measured value on Apple's global nav (blur(20px)+saturate(180%), superdesign.dev); 100% is the identity no-op.

brightness100%
%

Brightness adjustment on the transmitted backdrop. Default 100% is the identity point defined by Filter Effects L1 (research 02 §4 D1); dark-glass recipes go below 100% to dim what shows through and hold text contrast.

Code breakdown

Structure & DOM

backdrop-filter builds a three-layer stack: the backdrop (photo/gradient plus colorful display type) at the bottom, the frosted panel floating above it, and content on top. The essential difference from filter: it blurs everything rendered behind the panel while the panel itself is untouched.

<div class="scene">
  <img class="backdrop" src="photo.jpg" alt="" />
  <h2 class="display">Colorful display type</h2>
  <section class="frost">
    <h3>Frosted panel</h3>
    <p>Body copy and controls stay in normal flow and are never blurred.</p>
  </section>
</div>

Key points:

  • The frosted panel must be a sibling layer of the backdrop — it samples the rendered result behind itself;
  • The richer the backdrop (color, light/dark range), the stronger the frosted “thickness”; on a flat color the frost collapses entirely;
  • Panel text must survive contrast checks against both the brightest and darkest backdrop extremes.

Styling & Parameters

Three parameters map to one chained declaration, injected through --bf-* variables:

.frost {
  background-color: rgb(255 255 255 / 0.12);
  border: 1px solid rgb(255 255 255 / 0.3);
  border-radius: 20px;
  -webkit-backdrop-filter: blur(var(--bf-blur))
    saturate(var(--bf-saturate)) brightness(var(--bf-brightness));
  backdrop-filter: blur(var(--bf-blur))
    saturate(var(--bf-saturate)) brightness(var(--bf-brightness));
}

@supports not ((backdrop-filter: blur(1px)) or
  (-webkit-backdrop-filter: blur(1px))) {
  .frost { background-color: #e8ecf3; } /* raise fill for readability */
}

Where the numbers come from:

  • blur(20px): the glassmorphism.com generator’s classic recipe (research 06 §IV §16); Apple’s global nav measures 20px in production too;
  • saturate(180%): production CSS on Apple’s global nav measured blur(20px)+saturate(180%) (superdesign.dev); 100% is the identity no-op;
  • brightness(100%): the identity point defined by Filter Effects L1 (research 02 §4 D1) — only dark-glass recipes dip below 100%;
  • Support: Chrome 76 / Edge 17 / Firefox 103 / Safari 9 (BCD, research 02 §4 D2); Safari 9–17 needs the -webkit- prefix, unprefixed since Safari 18.

Motion & Interaction

  • Entrance: fade in + 10px rise, animating only opacity/translate; disabled under prefers-reduced-motion;
  • Frost is a static material — when backdrop content moves (scrolling, video), backdrop-filter re-samples every frame; large panels get expensive, so animate only transform/opacity (web.dev performance rules);
  • Live tuning: the three variables change the transmitted layer’s blur/vividness/brightness instantly, with no re-render of the panel content;
  • Hover-deepened shadows are optional and should be skipped on high-frequency controls (Apple HIG: avoid motion on frequent interactions).

Parameter Sensitivity

ParameterTurning it up…Turning it down…
blurthickens the frost and hides the backdrop; past 30px it smears into mush and sampling cost climbsapproaches a plain tinted block; below 6px the frosted feel disappears (production floor ≈ 4px)
saturateintensifies transmitted color, making the glass feel “alive”; past 250% it reads plastickythe backdrop eats the color and the glass turns gray; 100% is the identity point (production recipes always exceed 100%)
brightnesslifts the transmitted layer — on dark backdrops it can blow past text contrast; past 150% it washes outdims the transmitted layer, the standard dark-glass move; below 50% the glass nearly blackens

Working ranges: blur 10–25px, saturate 120–200%, brightness 80–110%; saturate and blur normally ship as a pair (the Apple nav recipe) — tuning one alone tends to unbalance the mix.

When to use 4
  • Floating nav bars and toolbars — cf. the production-measured Apple global nav blur(20px)+saturate(180%)
  • Overlay cards and players over colorful photos or gradients
  • Scrim layers under modals/drawers so covered content stays perceptible
  • Caption bars and badges on top of imagery
When not to use 4
  • Flat or white backdrops — nothing to blur, the effect collapses
  • Full-screen slabs — the backdrop re-samples every frame, cost scales with area
  • Small body text and dense data — the fill dilutes contrast, which shifts as the backdrop scrolls
  • Glass stacked on glass — layered translucency turns muddy
Accessibility

Check text on the panel against the brightest and darkest backdrop extremes for 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.3, research 06 §V §17); keep component boundaries at 3:1 non-text contrast (SC 1.4.11); backdrop scrolling shifts contrast continuously — firmer or darker fills hold up better than high-transparency ones.

Browser support

backdrop-filter: Chrome 76 / Edge 17 / Firefox 103 / Safari 9 (BCD-measured, research 02 §4 D2); Safari 9–17 requires the -webkit- prefix, unprefixed since Safari 18 (2024-09) (research 03 §1).

Fallback

Under @supports not (backdrop-filter: blur(1px)), raise the panel fill opacity (or switch to a solid surface) while keeping border and radius so text sits on a readable base; on Safari 9–17 also write -webkit-backdrop-filter.
