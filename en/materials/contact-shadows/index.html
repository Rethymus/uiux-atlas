Contact Shadows

接触阴影 · Contact Shadow · Ambient Occlusion · AO · 贴地阴影

The tight, dark elliptical shadow right beneath an element touching its surface, simulating ambient occlusion from 3D rendering; paired with float shadows (large offset, more diffuse) as a two-step grounded/floating elevation language.

Community ★★★
Live demo official default
Parameters
Geometry▾
distance6px
px

The fit distance between the element's bottom edge and the surface: the contact ellipse sits at that distance, height = 2×distance, protruding = distance (half tucked behind the element's bottom edge, half showing). Default 6px is the research recipe (ellipse bottom:-6px, height:12px, i.e. distance=6); 0 = fully grounded, the ellipse hides behind the element itself (physically correct). Larger distance = longer ellipse and the element reads as lifted.

spread6px
px

Blur radius of the contact ellipse (filter: blur). Default 6px is the research recipe blur(6px). This is the 'soft' in soft contact: larger = softer and more blended; too large smears into the surface and loses the sense of contact.

alpha0.25

Base opacity of the contact ellipse; default 0.25 is the research recipe hsl(... / 0.25). The float projection (Comeau layered recipe) scales on the same-opacity principle: each layer 0.2 = 0.25×0.8. Layering rule: equal opacity across layers, hierarchy comes from doubled offsets/blurs.

Color▾
color#3366cc

Shadow base color. Rule: never pure black — derive from the backdrop hue with reduced saturation and lightness; default #3366cc is the hex equivalent of the research example recipe hsl(220deg 60% 50%) on a blue backdrop. Tinted shadows read cleaner than black on light UI and stay in the backdrop's family.

Motion▾
modecontact

JS-type param; the demo subscribes and toggles data-mode. contact = grounded contact shadow (elliptical pseudo-element + blur, simulating AO — the element sits on the surface); float = floating projection (Comeau's five-layer doubling recipe: 1/2/4/8/16px offsets and blurs doubled in lockstep, equal opacity 0.2 per layer — the element floats above the surface). Both share the same shadow color and light ratio (horizontal:vertical = 1:2).

Code breakdown

Structure & DOM

A contact shadow is a two-layer “surface + floating element” structure where the shadow itself is a pseudo-element of the floating element: while the element touches the surface, a tight, dark, soft-edged elliptical shade sits directly beneath it, simulating ambient occlusion from 3D rendering; once the element floats, it switches to a large-offset, diffuse projection.

<div class="scene">
  <div class="floor"></div>      <!-- surface: where the shadow lands -->
  <div class="card">Content</div> <!-- floating element; ::after is the ground ellipse -->
</div>

Key points:

  • The ellipse hangs on the element’s ::after with z-index: -1 — above the surface, below the element, half hidden behind the element’s bottom edge;
  • The distance between element and surface drives the ellipse’s length and protrusion: more distance = longer ellipse, and the element reads as “lifted”;
  • Every element in a scene shares a single light source: one shadow hue, one offset ratio (e.g. horizontal:vertical = 1:2) — the premise of “the whole UI lit by one lamp” (research 12 §7).

Styling & Parameters

Four CSS-variable parameters plus one JS mode switch; the ellipse geometry and palette come straight from the research recipe:

.card::after {
  content: "";
  position: absolute;
  z-index: -1;
  left: 10%; right: 10%;                    /* ellipse insets, not full card width */
  height: calc(var(--cs-distance) * 2);     /* 12px @ default 6 */
  bottom: calc(var(--cs-distance) * -1);    /* protrudes 6px */
  border-radius: 50%;
  background: color-mix(in srgb, var(--cs-color) calc(var(--cs-alpha) * 100%), transparent);
  filter: blur(var(--cs-spread));           /* 6px */
}

/* float mode: Comeau's five-layer doubling projection, equal opacity per layer (default 0.2 = alpha 0.25×0.8) */
[data-mode="float"] .card {
  box-shadow:
    1px 2px 2px    color-mix(in srgb, var(--cs-color) calc(var(--cs-alpha) * 80%), transparent),
    2px 4px 4px    color-mix(in srgb, var(--cs-color) calc(var(--cs-alpha) * 80%), transparent),
    4px 8px 8px    color-mix(in srgb, var(--cs-color) calc(var(--cs-alpha) * 80%), transparent),
    8px 16px 16px  color-mix(in srgb, var(--cs-color) calc(var(--cs-alpha) * 80%), transparent),
    16px 32px 32px color-mix(in srgb, var(--cs-color) calc(var(--cs-alpha) * 80%), transparent);
}

Where the numbers come from:

  • Contact ellipse recipe: bottom: -6px + height: 12px + blur(6px) + hsl(shadow / 0.25) = the reusable snippet in research 12 §7 (distance=6 reproduces it exactly);
  • Layered float projection: offsets and blurs double 1/2/4/8/16px with equal opacity per layer — Comeau’s layering principle from the same research (0.2 per layer in the example);
  • Shadow base #3366cc = the hex equivalent of the research example hsl(220deg 60% 50%) (blue-backdrop example) — the concrete form of “never pure black”;
  • color-mix() is Baseline since 2023-05; box-shadow/filter are Baseline widely available.

Motion & Interaction

Contact-shadow motion serves the “elevation story” — lifting and landing:

  • Drag lift: distance grows while grabbed (the ellipse stretches and protrudes, the element rises), snapping back to grounded on drop; transition only on compositable properties like transform/filter;
  • Mode switch (contact↔float): swap the two shadow sets directly — no frame-by-frame animation, since the research explicitly warns that layered shadows are expensive to render (research 12 §7);
  • Shadows carry no interaction semantics: clickability is still expressed by cursors, focus rings and the usual indicators;
  • All transitions are disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.

Real-world examples

  • Josh Comeau’s blog (joshwcomeau.com): the origin of the layered tinted-shadow + ground-ellipse recipe; the author describes his card shadow system — same-opacity layered box-shadow plus a hue-derived shadow variable — in the source article of research 12 §7;
  • iOS 26 Liquid Glass: Apple’s qualitative rule that a glass element flexing/morphing into a larger one casts “a deeper shadow, more pronounced lensing” (WWDC25 219 [422–445s], research 09 §8.4) — the system-material counterpart of shadows deepening with volume and height;
  • WinUI/Fluent drag lift: “temporarily raise z while dragging, restore on drop” — the canonical Fluent depth interaction (see the theme-shadow entry, research 02 §C5); the contact↔float switch is its web implementation.

Working ranges: distance 4–8px (default 6 is the research recipe), spread 4–8px, alpha 0.2–0.3. Test the dark-surface × low-alpha combination deliberately — on dark surfaces the shadow is the first material cue to fail.

When to use 4
  • Cards picked up in drag-to-reorder: the contact shadow stretches and fades as they lift, snapping back to grounded on drop — the core prop of elevation storytelling
  • Bottom-docked elements (FABs, docks, toolbars) sitting convincingly on their surface
  • Metaphorical interfaces where objects rest on planes: 3D/AR views, product shelves, card tables
  • Distinguishing grounded vs floating cards — the two shadow styles contrast to encode height
When not to use 4
  • Frame-by-frame animation of layered float shadows — expensive to render, explicitly discouraged (research 12 §7)
  • Pure-black shadows — they gray out and detach from the backdrop (research 12 §7)
  • Mixing light sources — the whole product should share one light and one offset ratio (e.g. horizontal:vertical = 1:2)
  • Using shadows to mean clickable — shadows encode height and material, not interactivity
Accessibility

Shadows are decorative depth cues: never the sole carrier of hierarchy or state (pair with position/size/text); control boundaries still need 3:1 non-text contrast (WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.11); shadows lose legibility on dark surfaces — check against the real backdrop; shadows carry no interaction semantics, so focus indicators remain mandatory (WCAG 2.1 SC 2.4.7).

Browser support

box-shadow / filter / gradients are all Baseline widely available (research 12 §7); the demo's color-mix() needs Baseline 2023-05 (Chrome 111+ / Safari 16.2+ / Firefox 113+, research 12 §11). The color parameter itself is plain hex with no compatibility floor.

Fallback

Without color-mix(), fall back to 8-digit hex with a fixed alpha (e.g. #3366cc40); on very old engines without filter: blur, the ellipse degrades to flat translucent color (harder edge, still a valid contact shadow). Shadows are purely decorative — no degradation affects function or readability.
